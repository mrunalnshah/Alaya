# Alaya — positioning, names and copy

Everything here is written to be used, not admired. Copy-paste ready, with the reasoning under each
choice so you can change it knowingly.

---

## Part 1 — The one sentence

Before any tagline, the positioning:

> **Most money apps track what you spent. Alaya knows what you have.**

That is the whole difference, and it is defensible. Every finance app on Play does transactions. Almost
none of them know there are 400 g of paneer in your fridge, that the AC was serviced in March, that the
warranty on the television runs out in eleven months, and that with what is in the kitchen right now you
can make three of the six things you cook.

**Alaya is not a budgeting app with extras. It is a record of a household** — its money, its things, its
food, its upkeep — that happens to include a ledger.

Lead with that everywhere. The moment you position it as "expense tracker" you are competing with two
hundred apps on a feature you tied on.

### The name means something, and you should say so

**Alaya** — आलय — is Sanskrit for *abode, dwelling, storehouse, receptacle*. It is the second half of
**Himalaya**: *hima* (snow) + *ālaya* (abode of).

The product is a storehouse for a household, and the data never leaves the device — **it stays in the
abode**. That is not a retrofitted story; it is what the app does. Use it in the listing, use it on the
website, use it when somebody asks where the name came from.

## Part 2 — Punchlines

Ranked. The first three are the ones I would actually ship.

### Tier 1 — use these

> **Your home, remembered.**

Three words, works as a lockup under the logo, and it is the honest promise. Not "track" — *remembered*,
which is what a storehouse does.

> **Knows what you have. Not who you are.**

The privacy line, and the best one you have. It works because it is literally true: no account, no email,
no cloud, no analytics. Most apps cannot say the second half.

> **Everything you own. Nothing you shared.**

Sharper version of the same idea. Good for a store screenshot caption or a single-image ad.

### Tier 2 — good for specific slots

> **The abode that keeps count.** — website hero, leans on the name's meaning
> **Money, things, and what's for dinner.** — describes the actual scope in seven words
> **What's in the house, and what it cost.** — plain, benefit-first, no cleverness
> **Your kitchen already knows. Now you do too.** — for the recipe/inventory feature specifically
> **No account. No cloud. No catch.** — three-beat privacy line for a feature bullet
> **Offline by design, not by accident.** — for the technically-minded segment

### Tier 3 — for feature-specific copy

> **"Can I make this?" — answered.** *(recipes ↔ inventory)*
> **Two hundred rupees, and you remember why.** *(the quick-add note)*
> **A tablespoon of anything, in grams.** *(the density conversion)*
> **It says "I don't know" when it doesn't.** *(honest uncertainty — a real differentiator)*

### What I deliberately avoided

No "AI-powered" (it isn't, and claiming it invites a comparison you lose). No "smart" (means nothing). No
"effortless" or "seamless" (every app says both). No exclamation marks in the tagline — the product is
calm, and the copy should sound like it.

## Part 3 — The Play Store name

**Play gives you 30 characters and weights the title heavily in search.** "Alaya" alone is a mistake: it
is unsearchable, it collides with other apps and with a large yoga/wellness brand, and it tells a browsing
user nothing.

The rule is **Brand + the words people actually type.**

| Option | Chars | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `Alaya` | 5 | **No.** Invisible in search, ambiguous, no benefit stated |
| `Alaya - The App` | 15 | **No.** "The App" is filler that costs you 9 searchable characters |
| `Alaya: Expense Manager` | 22 | Safe, and throws away the differentiator — now you are app #201 |
| **`Alaya: Home & Money Manager`** | **27** | **Recommended.** Brand, both domains, and "manager" is what people search |
| `Alaya: Expenses & Inventory` | 27 | Strong alternative — more literal, slightly narrower |
| `Alaya — Money, Home & Pantry` | 28 | Most distinctive, but "pantry" is a low-volume search term |

**My recommendation: `Alaya: Home & Money Manager`**

It wins on search ("home manager", "money manager", "expense manager" all partial-match), states the
scope, and keeps the brand first so the name gets learned. Once you have installs and reviews you can
shorten toward `Alaya` — brand-only titles are earned, not chosen.

**Developer name: `WildeWulf`.** Keep it. It is distinctive, it is yours, and a separate publisher name
from the app name looks like a studio rather than a side project.

### Short description — 80 characters, and it matters more than the long one

Play shows it in search results and it is the single highest-leverage text field you have.

| Option | Chars |
|---|---|
| **`Track money, things and food at home. No account, no cloud, works offline.`** | **74** |
| `Your home's memory: expenses, inventory, recipes. Private, offline, no signup.` | 77 |
| `Money and inventory for your home. Everything stays on your phone.` | 66 |

**Use the first.** It names three things, then answers the question a privacy-minded browser is already
asking. "No account" is the highest-converting phrase you have, because every competitor demands one.

## Part 4 — Full store description

Paste-ready. Play allows 4,000 characters; this is about 2,200, which is the right length — nobody reads
4,000.

```
Most money apps track what you spent. Alaya knows what you have.

Your expenses, your groceries, your appliances, your recipes — one record of your
whole household, and all of it stays on your phone. No account. No cloud. No one
watching. 

No Ads if you don't want it!

WHAT IT DOES

• Money — Log an expense in about eight seconds: amount, what it was for, done.
  Income, transfers, and full itemised purchases when you want them. Multiple
  accounts, multiple currencies, and Indian lakh-crore formatting throughout.

• Inventory — What's in the house, how much of it, and when it expires. Batches
  with separate expiry dates, so the older milk gets used first.

• Recipes that read your kitchen — Add a recipe and Alaya tells you whether you
  can make it right now from what you have. Measure in tablespoons, teaspoons or
  cups; it converts to grams using the weight you set once per ingredient. Cook
  it, and the stock comes off your shelves automatically.

• Shopping lists — Built from what's running low, and turned into a purchase in
  one step when you get home.

• Recurring bills — Rent, subscriptions, the milkman. Alaya knows what's due and
  reminds you before it is.

• Appliances and upkeep — Warranties, service dates, who to call. It tells you
  the AC is due before the summer, not after.

• Analytics — Where the money went, by month, by category, by account. Tap any
  number to see the transactions behind it.

BUILT DIFFERENTLY

No account. There is no sign-up, no email, no password to forget. Open the app
and use it.

Works with no internet, forever. Not "offline mode" — offline is the only mode.
Nothing syncs because there is nowhere to sync to.

Your data is yours. One tap exports a full backup file you can put anywhere.
Nothing is uploaded, nothing is analysed, nothing is sold.

It admits what it doesn't know. When Alaya can't work something out — a
conversion it has no factor for — it says so instead of guessing. A wrong number
you trust is worse than no number.

Locks with a PIN or your fingerprint. Readable at double text size on a small
screen. Full screen-reader support.

FREE, AND HONEST ABOUT IT

Every feature above is free and always will be. There is an optional Support Us
screen with a small ad and a tip jar for anybody who wants to chip in. Nothing is
gated, nothing expires, and there is no subscription.

Alaya — आलय — is Sanskrit for the place where things are kept. It is the second
half of Himalaya: hima, snow, and alaya, abode. Your things stay in the abode.

Made by WildeWulf.
```

**Why it is shaped this way:** the differentiator is the first line, the features are scannable, and the
privacy claims come *after* the features — because somebody has to want the app before they care how it
handles data. Ending on the name's meaning gives reviewers and journalists something to quote.

## Part 5 — Screenshot captions

Play shows up to 8. Captions are read more than the description. One per screen, six words maximum.

| Screen | Caption |
|---|---|
| Dashboard | **Your whole household, one screen** |
| Quick add sheet | **An expense in eight seconds** |
| Recipe list with badges | **What you can cook, right now** |
| Ingredient row with the readout | **Tablespoons, converted to grams** |
| Inventory with expiry | **What's here, and what's expiring** |
| Analytics | **Where it actually went** |
| Backup screen | **Your data, in a file you keep** |
| Settings / palettes | **No account. Ever.** |

**Screenshot 3 should be your first.** The cookability badge is the thing no competitor has, and it is
visually obvious in a thumbnail. Lead with your difference, not your dashboard.

## Part 6 — The elevator pitches

**Five seconds:** "It's a money app that also knows what's in your fridge."

**Thirty seconds:** "Alaya keeps a record of a household — what you spent, what you own, what's in the
kitchen, when the appliances need servicing. The interesting bit is that they talk to each other: add a
recipe and it tells you whether you can make it from what's in stock, and cooking it takes the
ingredients off your shelves. It's entirely offline, there's no account, and nothing ever leaves the
phone."

**For an engineer:** "Flutter, drift over SQLite, offline-only, no backend at all. Integer minor units
for money and integer milli-units for quantity so there's no float arithmetic anywhere near a balance.
Layered architecture with a lint rule that fails the build if the UI imports the data layer."

**For an investor** — and read this before you use it: don't. A free, offline, no-account app with no
telemetry has no growth loop, no retention data and no monetisation beyond a tip jar. Those are
deliberate product choices and they are also exactly what a venture investor cannot underwrite. Pitch
this to *users*, not to capital.
